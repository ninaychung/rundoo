Three findings that shaped everything else

The rest of the analysis follows from these

A store doing $1.5–1.8m a year runs entirely on paper

No point of sale, no back-office system, no accounting software, and no customer list at all. Orders are handwritten, filed in a cabinet by install date, and archived when the job closes. Install scheduling lives in a book. The owner priced a flooring-specific system, was quoted six to seven thousand dollars up front plus monthly, and declined.

The obstacle is a capital decision, not a temperament

At roughly five to six jobs a week averaging six thousand dollars, five thousand a year in software is about three tenths of one percent of revenue — and it was still a no. That reframes the problem from product to price and inertia.

A trusted salesforce already visits every one of these stores weekly

Manufacturer reps restock the sample racks, pull discontinued boards, pitch new product, and negotiate discounts in the middle of a live quote. Asked how she hears about anything new, the store manager described a rep walking in with peel-and-stick tile. Whether a dealer would take a rep's word on software is the single untested question in this research.
